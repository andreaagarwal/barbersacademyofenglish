An Added Extra

English first — and its manners too, if you'd like them

The Academy is, first and last, an English school. But for students who want more than the words, Eddie can add the manners that go with them — an optional extra, woven into your lessons only if you ask for it.

Correct is not the same as right

You can compose a grammatically perfect sentence and still ask someone their salary at a drinks party, which no amount of grammar will save. Alongside the language itself, English social and business life runs on a quieter set of rules: manners, timing, understatement, the things said and the things carefully left unsaid. For students who want it, Eddie can weave a little of this into your English lessons — an added extra, never a substitute for learning the language well.

What etiquette lessons cover

  • Introductions, greetings and forms of address, from first names to "How do you do"
  • Small talk: what to raise, what never to raise, and how to leave a conversation alive
  • Table manners, dining and the full ceremony of afternoon tea
  • Invitations, hosting, thank-you notes and letters that charm
  • The unwritten rules: queuing, apologising, understatement, and the many meanings of "quite"

Learned at Repton, not from a manual

There are consultancies that will teach you etiquette from a laminated card. Eddie learned his at Repton and has practised it for sixty years, which is why his lessons cover not just the rule but the spirit of the thing: why the English apologise to furniture, what "interesting" means in a meeting, and when breaking a rule gracefully is the politest thing you can do.

Common questions

Do I have to take etiquette lessons?

No. The Academy is an English school first. Etiquette is an optional extra, added to your English lessons only if you ask for it.

What does British etiquette tuition cover?

Introductions and forms of address, small talk, table manners and afternoon tea, invitations and thank-you notes, and the unwritten rules of British life such as queuing and understatement.
